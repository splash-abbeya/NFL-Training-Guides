1 OVERVIEW

🎯 What Is the Entry Marketplace?

The Entry Marketplace is where players buy and sell entries — or shares of entries — in season-long Survivor contests that are already underway. A new player can buy their way into a contest that's already in progress, and an existing entrant can cash out before the contest ends by selling their entry, or part of it.

ℹ️ Scope at launch
The Marketplace currently supports Survivor contests only, and only select public contests are enabled — not every contest a member plays will show a Marketplace option.

3 FUNDAMENTALS

🔑 Key Concepts

Ownership shares

Ownership is always shown as a percentage (e.g., “You own 60%”). An entry can be owned 100% by one person or divided among co-owners, and each owner earns that same percentage of anything the entry wins.

  • Minimum share: 1%
  • Partial percentages (e.g., 1.5%) are not allowed

Pick control

Whoever owns 51% or more of an entry is the majority owner and controls its picks. Minority owners share in winnings but don't make picks. Buying or selling a majority share can move pick control from one person to another. (Full detail in Section 9.)

Splash Estimate (estimated value)

An entry's “Splash Estimate” is the contest's prize pool divided by the number of entries still alive.

ℹ️ It's a guide, not a guarantee
The Splash Estimate can lag live game action. It's meant to help members price listings and offers — it is never a guaranteed sale price.

Escrow

When a player makes an offer, funds are pulled from their wallet and held in escrow — not paid to the seller — until the offer is accepted, declined, or expires. If it isn't accepted, funds return to the buyer's wallet automatically.

Fees

Buyers pay a fee on top of the price; sellers have a fee deducted from the sale price. Fees are tiered by sale size, and the full breakdown is always shown before a customer confirms. (Full table in Section 7.)

4 TRADING

🛒 Buying an Entry

Two ways to buy

  • Buy Now — purchases the listing instantly at its asking price. The buyer is charged immediately (asking price + buyer fee), and the purchase is final.
  • Make an Offer — the buyer proposes their own price and share %. Funds are pulled and held in escrow while the seller decides.

A buyer can make an offer even on an entry that isn't actively listed for sale. The owner can accept, counter, or decline.

Counters

A seller can respond to an offer with one counter at a different price or share. The buyer can accept or decline the counter. If accepted, the buyer is charged the counter price plus the buyer fee.

💡 Talking to members
Remind members that Buy Now is instant and final — there's no changing your mind once it goes through. An Offer gives room to negotiate, but nothing is guaranteed until the seller accepts.

5 TRADING

💵 Selling & Listing an Entry

  • A seller can list a whole entry or just a share of it, at any price they choose.
  • Multiple listings are allowed for the same entry (e.g., two separate 20% shares).
  • Listing is free — nothing changes hands until someone buys, and the entry stays the seller's until it sells.
  • A listing can be canceled any time before it sells.
  • When a share sells, the seller is paid the sale price minus the seller fee, deposited to their Splash wallet.
  • Pricing a share well below the Splash Estimate triggers an informational warning — it does not block the listing.
⚠️ Majority sales move pick control
Selling 51% or more of an entry transfers pick control to the buyer. The listing flow discloses this to the seller up front — make sure members understand it before they list a majority share.

6 TRADING

🔄 The Offer Lifecycle

An offer moves through the following states:

StateWhat it means
LiveOffer open; buyer's funds held in escrow.
CounteredSeller proposed a different price/share; buyer can accept or decline.
AcceptedTrade settles instantly — share transfers, seller is paid, escrow released. Final.
DeclinedSeller passed. Buyer's escrowed funds are released back to their wallet.
ExpiredOffer timed out (no response, or hit the 90-minute pick-deadline cutoff). Funds released.
CanceledBuyer withdrew the offer before acceptance. Funds released.

Two timing rules that matter

  • A buyer can edit or cancel an open offer any time before it's accepted or expires.
  • An open offer must be accepted at least 90 minutes before the pick deadline — otherwise it expires automatically.
ℹ️ Why this exists
The 90-minute rule protects both sides from a trade landing too close to lock, when there wouldn't be time to make a pick.

8 TIMING

⏱️ Timing & Trading Windows

  • Trading opens only after the first slate, week, or day of games in a contest is complete — never before the contest has started.
  • All trading pauses 90 minutes before any game goes live, and stays paused for the entire game.
  • All open offers must be accepted at least 90 minutes before the pick deadline, or they expire automatically — meaning every week, all open offers will expire at the pick deadline.
⚠️ Example
90 minutes before Thursday Night Football, the Marketplace pauses. Once the game finalizes, the Marketplace reopens.

9 FUNDAMENTALS

🎮 Pick Control in Depth

Pick control is the concept customers most often misunderstand, so know it well:

  • The majority owner (51%+) makes the picks for the entry. There is always exactly one pick controller.
  • Selling 51% or more of an entry transfers pick control to the buyer as part of that trade.
  • When pick control transfers, the entry's current and upcoming picks are cleared. The new controller must make their own pick before the pick deadline — the previous owner's pick no longer stands.
  • Minority owners share in winnings but cannot make picks.
ℹ️ Minority-owner visibility
Before the pick deadline, minority owners cannot see the current week's pick — only whether a pick has been made. After the deadline, the pick becomes visible to everyone.

10 MONEY

🏦 Money: Wallet, Settlement & Payouts

  • All buying and selling runs through the player's Splash wallet. If a buyer is short on funds, they're prompted to deposit and returned to the same screen.
  • Settlement is instant — because offers are pre-funded through escrow, an accepted offer or Buy Now completes immediately: shares transfer, the seller is paid net proceeds, and escrow releases in the same moment.
  • Sellers receive the sale price minus the seller fee, which they can use to enter other contests or withdraw.
  • Winnings are split by ownership percentage if the entry wins.
  • Completed purchases and sales are final. Escrowed funds on declined, canceled, or expired offers are returned automatically.
💡 No waiting period
There's no multi-day settlement delay — net proceeds land in the seller's wallet the instant the sale completes.

11 GETTING STARTED

🔔 Notifications Customers Receive

Customers are notified in-app and by email for these Marketplace events:

  • Offer received (seller) — someone made an offer on their entry.
  • Offer accepted (buyer) — their offer was accepted and they now own the share.
  • Counter received (buyer) — the seller countered their offer.
  • Offer declined (buyer) — the seller passed; escrow released.
  • Offer expired (buyer) — the offer timed out; escrow released.
  • Entry sold (seller) — a sale completed, with net proceeds.

12 SUPPORT

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Getting started

Q: What is the Entry Marketplace?
A: It's where members buy and sell entries, or shares of entries, in contests that are already in progress — buying into a live contest, or cashing out before it ends.
Q: Which contests can members trade in?
A: Select public Survivor contests are enabled at launch. If a contest isn't shown in the Marketplace, it isn't available for trading.

Buying

Q: Can a member buy just part of an entry?
A: Yes — a full entry or a share of one. They own that percentage and earn the same percentage of anything the entry wins.
Q: What's the difference between Buy Now and making an offer?
A: Buy Now charges immediately at the listed price. An offer proposes a price with funds held in escrow — nothing is charged unless the seller accepts.
Q: Why does a member need money in their wallet just to make an offer?
A: Offers are pre-funded so a trade can settle instantly if accepted. The amount plus the buyer fee sits in escrow until then.

Selling

Q: How much will a seller actually receive?
A: The sale price minus the seller fee — 4% up to $25,000, 2% above that. The exact net amount is shown before listing and again at sale.
Q: Can a member cancel a listing?
A: Yes, any time before it sells.

Offers, counters & pick control

Q: A member's offer was declined — were they charged?
A: No. Declined offers are never charged — escrowed funds are released back to the wallet.
Q: A member bought a majority share and there's no pick set — why?
A: Pick control clears the entry's current and upcoming picks on transfer. The new controller must make their own pick before the deadline.

Money & timing

Q: Can a member get a refund on a purchase?
A: Completed purchases and sales are final and generally can't be reversed. Escrowed funds for a declined, canceled, or expired offer return automatically.
Q: Why can't a member trade right now?
A: Trading pauses while games are live and resumes once they finish. It also only opens after the first slate/week/day of a contest completes.
